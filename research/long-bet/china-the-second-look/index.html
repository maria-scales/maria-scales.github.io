China: The Second Look

Cheap and strong? Yes, in Chinese, or in English at a university employers know less: 16 mainland universities in the ARWU top 100, $5.4K–9.5K a year, and 74% of their world top-10 subjects taught only in Chinese.
Nov 2026 · Original research · Updated to ARWU 2026
TL;DR
  • 16 mainland Chinese universities are in the ARWU 2026 top 100, up from none in 2015. QS 2027 puts 6 of them in its top 100, THE 2027 puts 8.
  • A year on the Chinese track costs $5.4K–9.5K at 13 of 15 universities. With one or two language years first, the degree costs $32K–66K over five or six years.
  • Of the 110 places these universities hold in world top-10 subject rankings, 81 (74%) have no English-taught bachelor's. Across the catalogue, 916 of 1,022 programmes are taught only in Chinese.
  • Where English meets a top department, four years cost $52K–135K at known names and $24K–44K at Harbin IT, Southeast, Tianjin and Wuhan, which employers in the QS survey rank 183rd to 430th.
  • I look at the 18 Chinese universities in the ARWU 2026 top 100: 16 on the mainland and 2 in Hong Kong. Hong Kong runs on its own rules, so it gets a short section of its own. Three mainland universities entered the top 100 in 2026, Southeast, Harbin Institute of Technology and Tianjin (The Top 100 Is a Moving Target); I collected their data in November 2026.
  • The reader I have in mind is a family abroad choosing a STEM bachelor's degree: a passport from the EU, Turkey, Serbia, Russia, Armenia, Georgia or Kazakhstan, and a national school certificate or UK A-levels.
  • Prices are the published fees for the 2026 and 2027 intakes. I convert at the rate of 21 October 2026: CNY 6.68 per USD.
  • A study year is 10 months. Housing is the campus dormitory. Food and daily costs come from the university's own estimate; where a university publishes none, I use CNY 14,000 a year, the midpoint of other universities' estimates (8,000–20,000).
  • Subject strength is the university's world rank in the ShanghaiRanking subject tables (GRAS 2026). These tables measure research, so they say little about bachelor's teaching.

Families choosing a university abroad now hear two stories about China.

The first comes from the headlines. In June 2026 Zhejiang University replaced Harvard at the top of the Nature Index, which counts papers in leading science journals, and Chinese institutions took nine of its ten top places (Nature Index). Tsinghua is 11th in the Times Higher Education ranking for 2027.

The second comes from parents and students in forums. A student from Pakistan, admitted to computer science at a Chinese university, asks whether a Chinese degree will be worth anything abroad (r/China). Another thread asks why even Tsinghua and Peking degrees get dismissed online (r/ChinaLiuXueSheng).

Between the two stories sits the one thing almost everyone agrees on: China is cheap. Study agencies now list "studying in China is always cheap" among the myths they correct (Apply For China).

Fifteen minutes with the rankings

Spend fifteen minutes with the rankings and the first story wins.

In 2015 the ARWU top 100 had no university from mainland China. It had 2 in 2016, 4 in 2019, 8 in 2022, 13 in 2025 and 16 in 2026. Tsinghua moved from 101–150 to 18th. Times Higher Education, which also counts reputation and teaching, shows the rise too: 2 mainland universities in its top 100 in the 2016 edition, 8 in 2027. Tsinghua moved there from 47th to 11th. A year of study, with fees, dormitory and food, costs $5K–10K.

The impression is hard to avoid: this is the most promising direction in higher education right now, and a family should move fast.

You may already feel that pull.

Why read further

No surprise: as usual, it turned out to be way more complicated.

I went through the 18 Chinese universities in the ARWU top 100, 16 on the mainland and 2 in Hong Kong: fee pages, 1,022 bachelor's programmes, 57 subject rankings, language courses and the recognition rules after graduation. This article follows the full cycle a family goes through:

  • what the degree costs, year by year;
  • how long it takes;
  • whether the strong programmes are taught in a language your child speaks;
  • where the diploma leads after graduation.

Let's figure it out.

The short answer

Cheap and strong. Both are true, but not always together.

In engineering, chemistry and materials, Chinese departments hold most places in the world top 10. In mathematics and physics they hold none, in medicine 4 of 40 and in economics 1 of 40.

Cheap and strong come together in Chinese, or in English at a university whose name employers know less. To see why, look at universities subject by subject. ShanghaiRanking publishes a separate world ranking for each of 57 subjects, from mathematics to chemical engineering. When a university is among the ten best in the world in a subject, I call it a world top-10 subject for that university. Tsinghua, for example, is first in the world in chemistry and second in computer science, so both count.

Across the 57 subjects, the 16 mainland universities hold 110 such places. For 29 of them the university offers a bachelor's programme taught in English. The other 81, or 74%, can be studied only in Chinese. And the cheap route starts with one or two years of language school, so the degree takes five or six years.

The 29 English places split by price. 17 are at Tsinghua, Shanghai Jiao Tong, Zhejiang and Xi'an Jiaotong, where four years in English cost $52K–135K, or at universities with no published price. 12 are at Harbin IT, Southeast, Tianjin and Wuhan, 77th to 98th in ARWU, where four years cost $24K–44K. Employers abroad know these four names less: in the QS employer survey they rank 183rd to 430th, against 13th for Tsinghua (Table 12).

Why China drops off the shortlist

In my earlier article, What the World Actually Offers, China stayed unresolved. The first reason is the ranking families use; the second, language, comes later in this article.

ARWU puts 16 mainland universities in its top 100. QS 2027 puts 6 of them there, THE 2027 puts 8. A family that shortlists by QS sees 6 of the 16. Sichuan is 77th in ARWU and around 300th in QS; Xi'an Jiaotong is 79th and 296th; Southeast is 89th and around 335th.

The rankings measure different things.

ARWU counts mainly research, 90% of the score. Staff with Nobel prizes or Fields medals give 20%, highly cited researchers 20%, papers in Nature and Science 20%, all indexed papers 20%, and these scores divided by the number of staff 10%. The other 10% is alumni with Nobel prizes or Fields medals, which ARWU calls quality of education. It runs no surveys and measures nothing about teaching or current students.

HUST shows how this plays out. It is 72nd in ARWU and 307th in QS. In QS it gets the maximum score of 100 for citations per staff member. For academic reputation it gets 34, for staff per student 9, for international staff 6 and for international students 5 (QS 2027 indicator scores).

ARWU measures research, and this is where all 16 universities make the top 100. QS measures reputation and how international a university is, and on four of its indicators HUST gets between 5 and 34 out of 100. I read ARWU to judge the department and QS to judge how the diploma will read to an employer abroad.

A side-by-side of the four rankings, U.S. News included, is in Four Rankings, Four Questions.

A family that shortlists by QS drops China before it compares prices. Here is what the second look finds.

How to read this table

ShanghaiRanking's GRAS ranks universities separately in each subject, and each subject has its own world top 10. A cluster joins several subjects, so it has 10 places per subject. Chemical engineering and materials joins 5 subjects, which gives 5 × 10 = 50 places. Chinese universities hold 38 of them, US universities 7.

The GRAS subjects in each cluster:

  • Chemical engineering and materials: chemical engineering, materials science, nanotechnology, metallurgy, textiles.
  • Energy, mining, marine engineering: energy science, mining, marine engineering, remote sensing.
  • Civil and environmental engineering: civil engineering, environmental engineering, water resources, transportation.
  • Electrical and computer engineering: electrical and electronic engineering, telecommunications, automation and control, robotics, instruments.
  • Mechanical and aerospace: mechanical engineering, aerospace engineering.
  • Chemistry: chemistry.
  • Biomedical engineering: biomedical engineering.
  • Computer science and AI: computer science, artificial intelligence.
  • Earth and environmental sciences: earth sciences, atmospheric science, oceanography, ecology.
  • Biology: biological sciences, human biological sciences, biotechnology. GRAS has no computational biology, so I use these three as a proxy.
  • Mathematics and statistics: mathematics, statistics.
  • Physics: physics.

The split is sharp. In chemical engineering and materials China holds 38 of 50 places. In mathematics and physics it holds none. Medicine and economics are close to that: 4 of 40 and 1 of 40.

Computer science depends on what the ranking counts. By papers and citations (US News 2026–27) Tsinghua is first in the world, Peking second, Zhejiang third. By reputation among academics and employers (QS 2026) Tsinghua is 11th, Peking 16th, Shanghai Jiao Tong 30th and Zhejiang 34th. In my view both readings are fair. By research output, Chinese computer science is at the top of the world. By reputation, only Tsinghua and Peking are in the top 20; at the other universities the research is stronger than the name. For a computer science degree that employers abroad know by name, the list narrows to these two.

The rise is recent. ARWU scores each indicator in points from 0 to 100, and 100 points go to the strongest university in the world on that indicator.

  • Highly cited researchers: Tsinghua scored 0 points in 2013 and 70.9 in 2026.
  • Number of papers: the 16 Chinese universities average 81 points, US universities in the top 100 average 52.
  • Staff with Nobel prizes or Fields medals: the Chinese average is 0 points, the US average 41.

Papers show the research done now, and prizes reward work done decades ago. Chinese departments lead on the first and do not show on the second yet.

Tuition is close at 14 of 15 universities: CNY 16,000–34,000 ($2.4K–5.1K). Fudan charges up to CNY 42,000 ($6.3K). The dormitory makes most of the difference: from CNY 4,500 at Tianjin to 30,000 at Fudan ($0.7K–4.5K). The universities' food and daily estimates come to CNY 5,000–20,000 a year, or CNY 500–2,000 a month over a 10-month year ($75–300). Numbeo puts a resident's monthly costs without rent at CNY 3,200–4,100 ($480–610) (Numbeo). A student who has meals in the canteen spends less than an average resident, but the gap is large, so I read the universities' estimates as the lower bound.

The setback: language

What English leaves you

15 out of 16 mainland universities publish how many bachelor's programmes they offer international students in each language. Across them, 105 of 1,022 programmes are taught in English, or 10%; one more, at Shanghai Jiao Tong, is taught in French. Central South publishes only its English list, so I leave it out of the count. Harbin IT's campus in Shenzhen adds 8 English programmes with a separate admission; they are not in the 1,022.

  • No English-taught bachelor's: Peking (101 programmes), Sun Yat-sen (67), Nanjing (51), USTC (35).
  • About one programme in three in English: Harbin IT 30 of 97 (31%).
  • Up to one programme in five in English: Tsinghua 16 of 80 (20%), Shanghai Jiao Tong 10 of 53 (19%), Wuhan 11 of 68 (16%), HUST 5 of 45 (11%).
  • Fewer than one in ten: Zhejiang 9 of 95, Southeast 6 of 70, Sichuan 6 of 78, Fudan 5 of 67, Xi'an Jiaotong 4 of 48, Tianjin 3 of 67.

Why 11 of 16 universities
  • USTC runs no language course for degree applicants.
  • Zhejiang offers conditional admission with a language year but publishes no price.
  • Sichuan offers a language year only to CSC scholarship holders.
  • Sun Yat-sen: the only fee page dates from 2015 (CNY 17,400 a year).
  • Central South: CNY 14,000–20,000 a year on an undated page.

A language year is two semesters of about 18 weeks, 36 weeks in all. Where the universities publish the hours, it is 20 hours of class a week (Tsinghua, Nanjing, Tianjin; 20 to 24 at Central South), 16 to 36 hours in Fudan's preparatory programme and 1,188 hours over 36 weeks in Harbin IT's Program A. At 20 hours a week that gives about 720 hours of Chinese a year, a full-time course; Harbin IT's programme is denser, 33 hours a week.

  • Language courses, CNY 14,000–21,000 a year ($2.1K–3.1K) at HUST, Wuhan, Tsinghua, Nanjing, Tianjin and Southeast: they teach the language and give no route into the degree.
  • Preparatory programmes, CNY 37,200–56,000 ($5.6K–8.4K) at Peking, Shanghai Jiao Tong and Fudan: they end with the university's own exam, and passing it is the route into the bachelor's. Shanghai Jiao Tong and Fudan ask for HSK 3 at entry. Tianjin's Foundation Program, CNY 28,900 for one year, takes students from HSK 3 and leads to the university's own bachelor's.
  • Xi'an Jiaotong, CNY 25,800 ($3.9K), and Harbin IT, CNY 24,600 ($3.7K): the two preparatory programmes that openly take students from zero, 36 weeks to HSK 4 or 5. Harbin IT gives its graduates priority in admission, without a guarantee.

Either way, a language year costs close to a study year.

Daily life

  • Payments. A foreign Visa or Mastercard linked to Alipay or WeChat Pay covers up to $5,000 per payment. Cards from Russian banks do not work.
  • Internet. Google, YouTube and WhatsApp are blocked; VPN sits in a grey zone.
  • Winter. Of the twelve cities, Beijing, Xi'an, Tianjin and Harbin have central heating. In Harbin the January average runs from −22 °C at night to −12 °C in the day, and the heating season lasts about six months. In Nanjing and Hefei January is around 0 °C, and dormitories heat with air conditioning.
  • Travel. Hefei and Harbin have no direct flights from Europe, Turkey or Russia, so the trip goes through Shanghai or Beijing. Nanjing has one flight a week to Paris and one to Milan, Tianjin one to London Heathrow.
  • Visa. Short visa-free entry, which many of these passports now have, does not cover study. Every student needs an X1 study visa.

What a student without Chinese gets

By this point many families want to skip the language. Chinese is hard: one or two years of language school, paid on top of the degree ($4.7K–17.2K a year), before the first bachelor's lecture. So what does a student get who skips it and studies in English?

A student without Chinese has two ways into a world top-10 department. At a known name it costs $52K–135K for four years. At a less known name it costs $24K–44K. Strong, cheap and in English come together at universities employers abroad know less.

This is the part that surprised me most. Figure 1 and Table 1 at the start of this article show the split by university and name the subjects.

What English gives:

  • Mechanical engineering, fully open. All four of the 16 that are in the world top 10 (Tsinghua 1st, Harbin IT 3rd, Xi'an Jiaotong 4th, Shanghai Jiao Tong 9th) teach it in English. At Harbin IT four years cost $29.5K–30.7K, at Xi'an Jiaotong $121.7K–128.3K.
  • Twelve top departments on a cheap track, at four universities, $24K–44K for four years: Harbin IT 7 (instruments 1st, mechanical 3rd, AI and chemical engineering 5th, civil 6th, environmental engineering 7th, electrical 10th), Southeast 2 (civil 2nd, transportation 4th), Tianjin 1 (chemical engineering 2nd), Wuhan 2 (remote sensing 1st, geography 4th). Seven of the twelve are at Harbin IT, where an English class opens only if at least 20 students enrol; without that the programme runs in Chinese.
  • At the known names the top in English is expensive. 29 of the 110 world top-10 places have an English programme. 17 of these 29 cost $52K–135K for four years, or have no published price. Against the Chinese path at the same university, English costs 26% more at Zhejiang, 69% more at Tsinghua, about twice as much at Shanghai Jiao Tong's Global College and more than three times as much at Xi'an Jiaotong. I compare the middle of each range.

What you miss without Chinese:

Most of the catalogue. 916 of 1,022 bachelor's programmes at the 15 universities, or 90%, are taught only in Chinese. Among the departments that put these universities in the world top 10, 81 of 110 places, or 74%, have no English programme. In mathematics, physics and chemistry none of the 13 universities in Table 7 has an English programme; in computer science and AI there are 8 out of 72.

  • The strongest Chinese subjects. Chemistry, chemical engineering, nanotechnology, environmental engineering, metallurgy, AI, biotechnology, robotics and telecom hold 46 places in the world top 10 between them. English programmes: 4, all at Harbin IT and Tianjin (chemical engineering at both, AI and environmental engineering at Harbin IT).
  • Most of Tsinghua. It is world top 10 in 20 subjects: first in the world in chemistry, chemical engineering, materials and energy, second in AI and computer science. These six are taught in Chinese only; 15 of Tsinghua's 20 top-10 subjects have no English programme.

What Hong Kong changes

Two Hong Kong universities are in the ARWU top 100: HKU at 62nd and City University at 95th. For a family without Chinese they remove the language setback and add a different one.

  • Language. Both teach every bachelor's programme in English: 61 programmes at HKU, 45 at City University. No language year.
  • Admission. Thresholds are published in A-level grades. HKU expects around AAA for a BSc and AA*A for computing and data science; City University asks for grade E in three subjects as the formal minimum.
  • Price. A year costs $40K–47K with hall and living, five times the Chinese track on the mainland and close to a UK or Dutch university.
  • Departments. The two hold 9 places in the world top 10, 4 of them in STEM: geography (6th) and transportation (10th) at HKU, energy and metallurgy (both 9th) at City University. The rest are education, finance, library science, communication and public administration. Computer science is 32nd at HKU and 47th at City University, chemistry 101–150 and 30th, mathematics 301–400 at both.

The brand trap

Say the plan works. Two years of Chinese, four years in a world top-10 department, a diploma from a top-100 university. What does the graduate get next?

The department decides what the student learns. Abroad, the diploma is read by the university's name: the UK visa list includes 8 of the 16 universities, chosen by overall rank, and the Dutch orientation year asks for the top 200 in at least two of three rankings (IND). So a strong department is half of the choice. The other half is how the university's name reads after graduation.

Who recognises the degree. Before any visa or master's, a country has to accept the diploma as a bachelor's.

  • United States
    • There is no national authority: each university or employer decides (US Department of Education).
    • Evaluation services such as WES rate a four-year Chinese bachelor's as equal to a US one (WES).
  • United Kingdom
    • There is no legal recognition act. UK ENIC issues comparability statements, and universities such as Aberystwyth treat a four-year Chinese bachelor's as comparable to a UK one (UK ENIC, Aberystwyth).
  • Germany
    • All 16 are listed in anabin, the German recognition database, with status H+ and in its top category of excellence and 211 universities (anabin).
    • A four-year full-time Chinese bachelor's from them is rated "entspricht", equal to a German bachelor's.
  • France
    • There is no automatic equivalence. ENIC-NARIC France issues a comparability statement, and the university or the employer decides (étudiant.gouv.fr).
  • China
    • Every degree is checked in the national CHSI database, and foreign universities ask for its reports (Stanford, for one).

Who knows the name. Recognition says the degree is a bachelor's. The name decides whether an employer abroad notices it. In the Global Employability University Ranking 2026, a survey of 12,350 managers in 32 countries, Peking is 12th and Tsinghua 16th. The other 14 universities are outside the top 20 (THE and Emerging).

The QS employer survey goes further down the list. It ranks universities by how often employers name them, and QS publishes the rank up to 700th (QS 2027). Table 12 puts it next to the UK visa list and the price of a top department in English.

Where the degree opens a door. With a bachelor's from one of the 16, the largest job markets look like this:

  • Germany. All 16 are recognised, so two routes open: the Opportunity Card, a year to look for work without a job offer (€13,092 in savings, English B2 or German A1), and the EU Blue Card with a job offer from €45,934 a year for recent graduates (German Embassy).
  • United Kingdom. Here the name decides. The High Potential Individual visa gives two years without a job offer, but only to graduates of 8 of the 16: Tsinghua, Peking, Zhejiang, Shanghai Jiao Tong, USTC, Fudan, Nanjing and Harbin IT (GOV.UK). With a job offer from £41,700 a year, the Skilled Worker visa does not check the university (GOV.UK).
  • United States. Straight after a Chinese bachelor's, this door is mostly closed. The H-1B visa is a lottery, since 2026 weighted towards higher-paid jobs. A $100,000 fee for hiring from abroad, introduced in September 2025, is blocked by federal courts as of November 2026 and may return on appeal (University of Colorado ISSS). The realistic route is a US master's: OPT then gives 12 months of work, plus 24 in STEM fields, and the H-1B lottery keeps 20,000 places for US master's graduates.
  • China. With a bachelor's only, a work permit straight after graduation is open to graduates of Shanghai Jiao Tong and Fudan, for jobs in Shanghai's designated zones: the Free Trade Zone, Zhangjiang, Lin-gang and Hongqiao (Shanghai government).

So the degree works in Europe from the first year after graduation, and in the US mostly through a master's.

The name and the department can be far apart. A university's overall rank says little about any one of its departments.

Sichuan is first in the world in biomedical engineering and fourth in chemical engineering. In computer science, electrical engineering and physics it is outside the top 100. Nanjing, 70th in ARWU, is outside the top 100 in 18 of 37 science and engineering subjects. And 89 of China's 199 places in subject top-10 lists, or 45%, belong to universities outside the ARWU top 100 altogether: Beihang, Tongji, Northeastern, China University of Geosciences in Wuhan. A year earlier the share was 54%: Harbin IT and Southeast held 15 of these places before they entered the top 100 in 2026.

A family that picks Sichuan for its rank and enrols in computer science gets a department outside the world top 100. A family that picks Southeast for its civil engineering, 2nd in the world and taught in English, gets a name that employers place 430th. The practical rule: choose the department first, then check that the university's name opens the routes you need after graduation. For a cheap English programme at Harbin IT, Southeast, Tianjin or Wuhan, Germany and a master's abroad are open; a UK visa without a job offer is open only from Harbin IT.

So, a second look

Is China worth a second look? For a family ready to learn Chinese, yes. For a family ready to trade a known name for a top department, also yes.

  • Strong. In engineering, chemistry and materials, Chinese departments hold most places in the world top 10: 38 of 50 in chemical engineering and materials.
  • Cheap. A year costs $5.4K–9.5K at 13 of 15 universities. The whole path, with one or two language years, costs $32K–66K.
  • Chinese, or a less known name. 81 of the 110 world top-10 places have no English programme. Of the 29 that have one, 17 cost $52K–135K for four years at known names. The other 12 cost $24K–44K at Harbin IT, Southeast, Tianjin and Wuhan, which employers in the QS survey rank 183rd to 430th.
  • After graduation the name matters. In the US, the largest job market, there is no national recognition: evaluation services such as WES rate a four-year Chinese bachelor's as equal to a US one, and in 2024 Peking was 5th in the world, US universities included, by the number of its bachelor's graduates who went on to a US research doctorate (NSF). Germany rates all 16 equal to a German bachelor's. Of the 16, employers abroad put two names in the global top 20, Peking and Tsinghua.

The practical rule: decide on the language first. With one or two years of Chinese, choose a department in the world top 10, then check how the university's name reads for the routes you need. Without Chinese, decide on the name. A known name gives mostly mechanical, electrical and civil engineering, at $59K–128K for four years. A less known name gives 12 top departments, from instruments at Harbin IT to chemical engineering at Tianjin, at $24K–44K, with Germany and a master's as the routes after graduation.
