Four Rankings, Four Questions

Tsinghua is 6th in one global ranking and 18th in another. What ARWU, QS, THE and U.S. News count, and which one to read for which question.
Nov 2026 · Note
TL;DR
  • Tsinghua is 18th in ARWU 2026, 14th in QS 2027, 11th in THE 2027 and 6th in U.S. News 2026–2027. HUST ranges from 72nd in ARWU to 307th in QS.
  • Of the 16 mainland Chinese universities in the ARWU top 100, U.S. News puts 10 in its top 100, THE 8 and QS 6.
  • Reputation surveys are 45% of the QS score, 33% of THE, 25% of U.S. News and 0% of ARWU. ARWU gives 30% to Nobel prizes and Fields medals.
  • To judge a department, read a research ranking by subject (ShanghaiRanking or U.S. News). To judge how the name reads to an employer abroad, read QS or THE.
  • I compare four global rankings: ShanghaiRanking ARWU 2026, QS World University Rankings 2027, Times Higher Education (THE) World University Rankings 2027 and U.S. News Best Global Universities 2026–2027.
  • Weights come from each ranking's published methodology. Where I could not open the publisher's own page, I name the secondary source.
  • My test case is the 16 mainland Chinese universities in the ARWU 2026 top 100, the same group as in China: The Second Look. All 16 are in the ARWU top 100 and 6 are in the QS top 100, so the gap between rankings is wide here.

Why one university gets four different places

Tsinghua is 18th in ARWU, 14th in QS, 11th in THE and 6th in U.S. News. HUST is 72nd in ARWU, 92nd in U.S. News, 191st in THE and 307th in QS.

Neither number is wrong. Each ranking asks a different question and gives each answer a different weight.

ARWU: research output and prizes

ARWU counts research and prizes. Staff with Nobel prizes or Fields medals give 20% of the score, alumni with them 10%, highly cited researchers 20%, papers in Nature and Science 20%, all indexed papers 20%, and these scores divided by the number of staff 10% (ARWU methodology). There are no surveys and no data about students.

So a large university that publishes a lot climbs, even without prizes. That is the main way Chinese universities rose: across the 16, the average 2026 score for publications is 81 out of 100, against 52 for the 37 US universities in the top 100; for staff Nobel prizes it is 0 against 41. The known weak sides are teaching, the humanities and size: only the 10% per-staff indicator adjusts for how big a university is.

QS: reputation and international mix

QS gives 45% to two reputation surveys: academics name the best universities in their field (30%), employers name the universities whose graduates they prefer (15%). Citations per staff member give 20%, staff per student 10%, and the share of international staff, international students and international research links 15%. Graduate outcomes and sustainability give the last 10%.

So QS rewards universities that people already know and that teach many foreigners. HUST gets the maximum 100 for citations per staff member, and 34 for academic reputation, 9 for staff per student, 6 for international staff and 5 for international students (QS 2027 indicator scores). That lands it at 307th.

THE: a mix, with a third from surveys

THE splits its score into teaching (29.5%), research environment (29%), research quality (30%), international outlook (7.5%) and industry (4%). Reputation surveys sit inside the first two and add up to 33%. Citation impact is 15%.

THE sits between ARWU and QS. It places 8 of the 16 mainland universities in its top 100, and Tsinghua is 11th.

U.S. News: research, measured many ways

U.S. News builds 65% of the score from publications and citations, split into nine measures: papers, books and conference papers, how often they are cited, and how many papers sit among the 10% and the 1% most cited in the world. Research reputation in the world and within the region gives 25%, international collaboration 10%. Both the survey and the paper counts come from Clarivate. The 2026–2027 edition ranks 2,250 universities.

Like ARWU it rewards research volume, and it also weighs how often papers are cited and adds a reputation part. It places 10 of the 16 in its top 100. The other six are Harbin IT at 111th, Xi'an Jiaotong at 129th, Central South at 131st, Southeast at 133rd, Sichuan at 139th and Tianjin at 166th.

Which ranking to read for what

Your question What to read Why
Is this department strong in research? ShanghaiRanking subject tables (GRAS) or U.S. News subject rankings they count papers and citations in the subject
Will an employer abroad know the name? QS, THE surveys are 33–45% of their score: academics in THE, academics and employers in QS
Will my child study with many international students? QS international student ratio THE and QS publish it as a separate score
How many staff per student? QS faculty-student ratio, THE staff-to-student ratio the closest measure of teaching in global rankings
Will the diploma qualify for a visa? the ranking the visa names the Netherlands uses THE, QS and ARWU; Hong Kong uses the top 100 of THE, QS, U.S. News and ARWU, plus the top 20 mainland universities

Table 3. Which ranking answers which question.

Three practical rules:

  • Read at least two rankings of different types, one research ranking (ARWU or U.S. News) and one reputation ranking (QS or THE). If a university is high in a research ranking and low in a reputation ranking, it publishes more than its name carries abroad; HUST is the example above.
  • For a bachelor's, read the subject ranking. An overall rank says little about any one department; a subject rank describes the department your child will join.
  • Global rankings say little about bachelor's teaching. Staff per student is the closest proxy. Ask the university for class sizes and graduate destinations.
