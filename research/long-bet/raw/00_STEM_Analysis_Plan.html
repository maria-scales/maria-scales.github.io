Normalise & Categorise
Clean the raw data before any analysis
Data prep
▶
  • 🏷️
    Unify program names
    Map synonyms to canonical labels: "Computing" = "Computer Science" = "Informatics"; "Aeronautics" = "Aerospace Engineering"; "Statistics" = "Statistical Science". Build a synonym table so the same discipline is never double-counted.
  • 📦
    Assign subject clusters
    Group all programs into ~10 clusters: CS & AI · Mathematics & Statistics · Physics & Astronomy · Chemistry · Engineering (Civil / Mech / Elec / Aero / Chem) · Bioengineering & Bioinformatics · Data Science · Materials Science · Earth & Environmental · Interdisciplinary.
  • 🎓
    Tag degree levels
    Flag each entry as UG only / PG only / UG+PG. Note integrated master's (MEng, MSci, MPhys, MMath) vs. separate postgraduate programmes. This matters for application planning.
Deliverables
Cleaned programme spreadsheet (.xlsx) Synonym / normalisation table Subject cluster taxonomy
2
Coverage Matrix
See what's offered where, at a glance
Quantitative
▶
  • 📊
    University × Subject grid
    Build a 100-row × ~30-column matrix. Each cell = ✓ UG, ✓✓ UG+PG, PG, or — (not offered). Immediately shows which programs are universal (CS, Maths) and which are rare (Molecular Engineering, Computational Neuroscience UG).
  • 📈
    Frequency ranking
    Count how many of the 100 universities offer each subject. Rank from most common (Computer Science: ~99) to rarest (e.g. Engineering Mathematics: ~8). A frequency chart makes this immediately readable.
  • 🗺️
    Geographic distribution
    Which subjects are strongest in which regions? Financial Mathematics dominates UK & Switzerland; Nuclear Engineering is strong in USA, China, France; Interdisciplinary Sciences are a Swiss/UK speciality. A country heatmap reveals these patterns.
Deliverables
Coverage matrix (Excel / interactive) Frequency ranking chart Country distribution heatmap
3
Uniqueness & Gaps
Find what's rare, distinctive, or missing
Comparative
▶
  • 💎
    Rare programs (offered by <10 universities)
    Identify niche offerings: Engineering Mathematics (Bristol, Bath), Molecular Engineering (UChicago, Caltech, MIT), Mathematical Engineering & Information Physics (Tokyo), Interdisciplinary Sciences (ETH Zurich), Computational Neuroscience UG (UCL, Bristol). These signal a university's distinctive identity and can be strong differentiators in applications.
  • 🔀
    Interdisciplinary programs
    Flag programs that intentionally cross clusters: Natural Sciences (Cambridge, UCL), Computational Science & Engineering, Cognitive Science, Data Science as standalone UG, Bioinformatics at UG level. Map which universities offer these at undergraduate entry — they're rarer than they appear.
  • ❌
    Coverage gaps by country
    Most Chinese universities don't offer standalone AI or Data Science UG (it's embedded in CS). Most UK universities have no Operations Research UG. Most European universities have no Financial Engineering UG. Gaps are as informative as presences when choosing where to apply.
Deliverables
Rare programs shortlist Interdisciplinary program map Gap analysis by country

Subject-Level Rankings
Best universities per subject, filtered by region
Decision support
▶
  • 🏆
    Best universities per subject cluster
    Cross-reference ARWU rank with program availability to produce ranked lists like "Top 15 universities offering Applied Mathematics at UG level" or "Top 10 for standalone AI BSc". Note: ARWU rank ≠ subject-specific rank — it is a useful but imperfect proxy.
  • 🌍
    Best-by-country per subject
    For each subject cluster, identify the top 2–3 universities in each major country or region. Useful when geography, language, tuition cost, or visa requirements constrain the choice set.
  • 🔗
    Career & postgraduate pathway mapping
    From each cluster, map common career pathways: CS → software engineering, ML research, quant finance; Applied Maths → actuarial science, research, data science; Bioinformatics → genomics, drug discovery, biotech. Helps evaluate degree "fit" beyond just the subject name.
Deliverables
Top-N tables per subject Country-filtered shortlists Career pathway diagram
5
Personal Shortlist
Filters applied → 10–20 target universities
Action
▶
  • 🎯
    Filter by Andrey's priorities
    Once subject preferences are confirmed (e.g., "CS + Maths, open to Data Science, prefer Europe or UK"), apply filters to the coverage matrix. The result is a personalised list of 10–20 universities across reach, target, and safe tiers.
  • 📋
    Entry requirements check
    For each shortlisted program, research required A-levels or equivalent qualifications. Cross-reference with the Edexcel A-level files already in the folder (Mathematics, Further Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology). Flag any admissions tests required: MAT, PAT, ESAT, TMUA, SAT/ACT.
  • 📄
    Final report
    Produce a single Word or PDF document summarising: subject cluster analysis, shortlisted universities with rationale, entry requirements per program, and next steps (open days, application deadlines, entrance exam preparation).
