📘 How to read A-level offer grades

A*A*A means three A-level grades — one per subject. Grades are listed in descending order by convention (highest first), but the order itself doesn't matter. What matters is hitting the required grades in the right subjects — for example, Cambridge CS requires the A* specifically in Mathematics, not just anywhere across your three subjects.

Yes, 3 A-levels is the standard. UK students typically sit exactly 3 A-levels in Year 13 (some do 4, but universities don't require it). The offer is made on those 3. So A*A*A is the highest possible standard 3-subject outcome, since A* is the top grade.

Offer What it means Approx. difficulty
A*A*A Two A*s + one A across 3 subjects Extremely competitive — top ~5% nationally
A*AA One A* + two As across 3 subjects Very competitive — top ~10% nationally
AAA Three As across 3 subjects Competitive — top ~20% nationally

The A-level grade scale from highest to lowest: A* → A → B → C → D → E (all passing), then U (ungraded/fail). Only 2 of the top 10 ARWU universities (Cambridge #4, Oxford #6) publish A-level grade requirements. The 8 US universities in the top 10 (Harvard, MIT, Stanford, Berkeley, Princeton, Columbia, Caltech, Chicago) use a holistic SAT/ACT-based process and do not publish A-level offer grades.

🇺🇸 Applying to US universities with A-levels

A-levels are valued highly by US universities — they signal academic rigour above the standard US curriculum. However, the application system is entirely different from UCAS.

Applications go through Common App (used by ~1,000 US universities). Most students apply to 10–15 schools simultaneously, with a deadline of January 1 (regular) or November 1 (early).

Requirement Details
SAT / ACT Test-optional at most top universities (Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Columbia, Penn, Brown, Dartmouth). MIT has reinstated the requirement. Strong scores help if submitted; skipping is fine at test-optional schools when A-level grades are strong.
Essays The biggest difference from UK admissions. One main personal essay (~650 words) via Common App, plus 2–8 supplemental essays per university. Most time-consuming part of the application.
Recommendations 1 letter from school counsellor + 2 from teachers (A-level subject teachers work well).
Activities list Extracurriculars, competitions, research, volunteering. US admissions weighs this heavily alongside grades.
Transcripts A-level predicted grades submitted with the application; actual results sent after exams. UK schools know how to handle this.
English test TOEFL or IELTS usually required for non-native English speakers. Some universities waive it if A-level English was taken or if the applicant attended an English-medium school for several years.

Not needed for US universities: No subject-specific admissions tests (MAT, PAT, STEP — those are UK-only) · No UCAS personal statement · No conditional offer based on predicted grades — US universities give unconditional offers or rejections. Note: top US universities have very low acceptance rates (3–7% for Harvard / MIT / Stanford) and evaluate applications holistically — grades are one factor among many.
